Escape room reset checklist

Thirty-odd things, seven minutes, one shot.

Between bookings your game master has to relock the padlocks, re-shelve the props, rewind the mechanism, and put the combination back to where it started. Miss one and the next team hits a dead end that isn't part of the game. This app is that checklist, with a photo of what right looks like beside every step.

Existing game-master platforms reset your electronic puzzles from a PC in the control room. Nothing covers the physical half — the padlocks, the drawers, the props that walked off in someone's pocket. Escape Room Reset is deliberately only that half, and it is the half that costs you reviews.

Author once, run it forever

Set the room up the way your best game master does it, and everyone else inherits their standard.

1

Photograph the right state

Each step gets reference photos of what correct looks like — the wheel at K, the drawer shut, the key back on its hook.

2

Run it in reverse

Steps play in reverse solve order, the way a room actually gets reset, on big photo-first cards built for a shared device.

3

Double-check, then sign

A second confirmation sweep is required before anyone can sign off with their initials. The log records who and when.

Built for the game master station

One iPad on the counter, shared by every shift. No logins to hand out and nothing to remember.

Every room you run

Colour-coded rooms with their step counts, and when each was last reset and by whom, right on the list.

Proof for the owner

Every reset logged with its duration against target and the initials of whoever signed it. Weekly PDF and CSV export.

When props go missing

Report a missing or broken prop mid-reset with a photo and a note. It stays visible until somebody fixes it.

Details that survive a busy Saturday

A timer with a target

Your turnaround goal is on screen the whole time, so a slow reset is visible while it is still happening.

Accountable sign-off

Initials, optionally protected by a PIN, so a signed reset means a specific person stood in that room.

Backup in one file

Every room, step, photo, and log in a single file you can carry to a replacement device.

Completely offline

No accounts, no cloud, no tracking, no subscription. The venue Wi-Fi going down is not your problem.

What it deliberately isn't

Escape room software usually tries to be everything. This does one job so it can be trusted to do it.

Not a booking system

It never touches your calendar, your customers, or your payments. Keep whatever you already use.

Not a prop controller

It cannot trigger a maglock or reset an electronic puzzle. It tells a human what to do with their hands.

Not a hint system

Nothing here runs during a game. It lives in the gap between one team leaving and the next arriving.

Not a multi-device sync

The log lives on the device that recorded it. That is why backup is one file and why it works with no network at all.
