Road rage, logged and let go

That light is not long. It is personal.

Three minutes at the same intersection, third time this week, and there is nowhere to put that feeling except the horn. StopRaging gives it somewhere else to go: one tap, the intersection is recorded, and the app immediately asks you to breathe. The anger becomes a data point instead of a lane change.

Tap, breathe, drive

The entire interaction is designed to be over before the light changes.

1

One tap

Your location is captured, the intersection is identified, and the event is stamped with the time and the part of day. A haptic confirms it. That is the whole input.

2

A five-minute cooldown

Log the same intersection twice inside five minutes and the app declines, politely. Rage-tapping a counter is not catharsis, and it would poison the map for everyone else.

3

Then it changes the subject

The screen turns from red to deep blue and a circle starts breathing at four seconds in, four seconds out. You are welcome to ignore it. Most people do not.

Proof that it is not just you

Every logged event joins a shared map. Intersections that grind people down show up hotter than the ones that do not — and suddenly the light you have been swearing at alone has forty other witnesses.

The part that actually helps

A breathing circle, not a lecture

CarPlay, hands where they belong

Log from the car display and get the same breathing prompt on the dashboard, so the phone stays in the cupholder.

It works with no signal

Events queue on the device and sync when you are back on the network. A tunnel does not lose your tap.

Your history is yours

Your own logs in one list, and an account you can delete outright from Settings — with the app telling you plainly what deletion removes.

The fine print, up front

An app about anger while driving has to be careful about what it encourages.

Not while moving

StopRaging is for a car that is stopped. Nothing in it needs your attention at speed, and CarPlay exists precisely so the phone stays down.

Not a traffic authority

The map shows where people felt frustrated, not where signals are mistimed. It is a record of feelings, and it is honest about being one.

Not therapy

A breathing exercise is a small tool for a small moment. If anger behind the wheel is a pattern for you, this app is not the answer to it.

Coarse locations only

Events attach to an intersection, not to you. There is no trip history, no route, and no public profile.
